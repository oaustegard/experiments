## 1. Layer-wise ablation of attention masks in BERT-style encoders

**DecBERT: Enhancing the Language Understanding of BERT with Causal Attention Masks**
Ren, He, Guo, Yang, Sun (Findings of NAACL 2022). arXiv:2204.08688 / aclanthology.org/2022.findings-naacl.89
The closest existing analogue to the proposed per-layer causal-mask ablation. They add a causal attention mask to a *subset* of layers (their headline configuration adds it to the **first two layers**, leaving the rest bidirectional) as a substitute for explicit position embeddings, on the logic that lower layers encode positional/structural information and a causal mask is a "free" way to inject word-order signal there. Numbers: with no position embeddings at all, plain BERT reaches pretraining perplexity 353.97; adding causal masks in the low layers drops that to **4.59** — i.e., near parity with position-embedded BERT. With position embeddings kept, DecBERT still gets a small net GLUE improvement and faster pretraining convergence at matched compute. This is evidence that (a) causal masking in a *few* early layers of an encoder is not catastrophic and can even help, and (b) full-stack causal masking (GPT-style) is reported elsewhere in the same paper's related-work discussion as clearly worse than BERT on GLUE — so the effect is genuinely layer-position-dependent, not just "any causal masking is fine." This paper never runs the reverse ablation (causal-mask one layer at a time, measure MLM loss delta) that our experiment does; it only tested one hand-picked prefix configuration.

**"What does BERT look at?"-style probing and general BERTology on layer roles**
No paper found that runs the *exact* protocol "causally mask layer i alone, measure ΔMLM-loss, sweep i" — search for this specific ablation surfaces only tangential material (LTG-BERT / AntLM's alternating CLM+MLM training scheme, which trains layers under mixed objectives rather than ablating masks at inference/eval time). This is a genuine gap (see Delta).

**UniLM-style prefix/mixed masking (background, not directly retrieved with numbers this pass)**
UNVERIFIED — not fetched this pass, but well-known as prior art: UniLM (Dong et al. 2019) trains a single Transformer under multiple self-attention masks (bidirectional, left-to-right, seq2seq) via a per-example mask-type selection rather than a per-layer split. It establishes that mask pattern can be varied without separate architectures, but it varies mask *type per training example*, not *per layer within one forward pass*, so it doesn't inform the layer-localization question directly.

## 2. Attention sinks / anchor tokens / registers

**What Does BERT Look At? An Analysis of BERT's Attention**
Clark, Khandelwal, Levy, Manning (ACL BlackboxNLP 2019). arXiv:1906.04341 / aclanthology.org/W19-4828
Directly supports the "anchor token" premise for design (b)/(c). Finding, with numbers: **over half of the attention in layers 6–10 focuses on [SEP]**; more broadly, "more than half of a head's total attention is given to special tokens" — **[CLS] dominates in early layers, [SEP] in middle layers, and punctuation ([.]/[,]) in deep layers**. Some heads have near-perfect (reported as "remarkably high accuracy") correspondence to syntactic relations (direct objects, determiners, prepositional objects) and coreference. This is the strongest existing evidence that in an encoder, attention mass is *not* spread uniformly but concentrates on a small, identifiable set of token types/positions — precisely the anchor-token hypothesis in design (b)/(c) — though it was measured on standard bidirectional BERT-base/large, not under any masking ablation, and it doesn't measure how much *task loss* depends on that concentration.

**Efficient Streaming Language Models with Attention Sinks (StreamingLLM)**
Xiao, Tian, Chen, Han, Lewis (ICLR 2024). arXiv:2309.17453 (github.com/mit-han-lab/streaming-llm)
Establishes the causal-LM attention-sink phenomenon: decoder-only LMs assign disproportionate attention to the **first few tokens (as few as 4 initial tokens)** regardless of semantic content; keeping only those sink KVs plus a sliding window preserves near-normal performance up to **4M+ tokens**, with up to **22.2x speedup** over sliding-window recomputation. Directly analogous mechanism to what design (b) proposes (restrict long-range attention to a small anchor set) but demonstrated for autoregressive decoders and for a *cache-efficiency* purpose, not for recovering bidirectional MLM loss. Relevant caveat for design: sink identity there is positional (first-token bias from softmax/training dynamics), which may or may not coincide with the *semantic* anchors (CLS/SEP/punctuation) BERT literature identifies — our design should check whether ModernBERT's future-attending mass lands on positional sinks, semantic anchors, or both.

**Vision Transformers Need Registers**
Darcet, Oquab, Mairal, Bojanowski (ICLR 2024, arXiv:2309.16588 — not directly fetched this pass, summarized via secondary sources) and follow-up **Vision Transformers Don't Need Trained Registers** (Jiang, Dravid et al., arXiv:2506.08010, fetched via search).
Original registers paper: found "high-norm tokens" appearing in low-informative background patches of trained ViTs, hypothesized as the model repurposing uninformative patches as compute buffers for global information ("massive activations" localized to a few token slots); adding a handful of learned register tokens removes the artifact. The 2025 follow-up shows this is training-free-fixable: register-like high-norm ("sink") neurons can be identified post hoc and their activation shifted onto an added untrained token, without retraining. Together these establish, in a bidirectional (ViT) encoder, that a **small number of token slots absorb disproportionate global-routing activity**, which is the vision-domain analogue of the anchor/sink hypothesis in design (b)/(c). UNVERIFIED: exact percentage of tokens/heads affected — figures were not retrievable from the fetched pages; only the qualitative claim is confirmed.

**Attention Sinks in Diffusion Language Models**
Rulli et al., arXiv:2510.15731 (2026).
Most relevant single source for thread 2+3 combined, since it studies sinks specifically in bidirectional/diffusion MLM-style models (LLaDA-8B, Dream-7B, MMaDA-8B) rather than causal LMs. Key numbers: masking **one** sink token causes **<1%** performance drop in DLMs (vs **98%** catastrophic loss for masking one sink in autoregressive Llama-3.1-8B on GSM8K/HumanEval); masking **5** sinks costs only **1–3%**; masking **10** sinks costs up to **~17%** (worst case, MMaDA-8B on HumanEval) — i.e., DLM sinks are far more redundant/robust than AR sinks. Also: sink *positions shift dynamically* during the denoising process — rightward in LLaDA-8B, right-to-left in Dream-7B, largely static in MMaDA-8B — and **sink count per layer decreases with depth, converging to 1–2 sinks per layer** in deeper layers of LLaDA-8B. This is strong, directly-transferable evidence for design (c): in a bidirectional/diffusion setting, "future-context" attention mass is not just concentrated but is also *cheap to ablate* without much loss — supporting the idea that a small anchor set could preserve most of bidirectional MLM's benefit. It also warns that anchor identity is **not static across the generation/inference process**, a pitfall for any fixed-anchor-set design.

## 3. AR→bidirectional / AR→diffusion adaptation varying which layers become bidirectional

**dQwen3.5: Hybrid-Attention Diffusion Language Models**
arXiv:2609.20751 (2026); also referenced as github.com/AntonXue/dQwen.
This is the paper motivating the experiment, and it is the single most load-bearing source for thread 3. Architecture: Qwen3.5's stack interleaves Gated DeltaNet (linear-attention/RNN, causal-by-construction) layers with full-softmax-attention layers; **attention layers are 25% of the stack (6/24 layers at the two smaller sizes, 8/32 at the two larger)**, with a **full-attention layer every 4th layer** by default; the rest (~75%) are Gated DeltaNet. dQwen3.5's adaptation recipe **bidirectionalizes only the attention layers** (disables their causal mask) and **leaves the Gated DeltaNet layers causal, unmodified** — explicitly stated rationale: "causality in a GDN is built into its recurrence rather than imposed by an attention mask, and so bidirectionalization would require non-trivial architectural modifications." Model sizes: 0.8B, 2B, 4B, 9B (trunk sizes 0.50B/1.37B/3.57B/6.92B). Training-efficiency number: against a full-attention AR control at matched ~1.4B trunk size, the hybrid **reaches a given diffusion-training loss using a median 2.21x fewer content tokens** than the full-attention control needs (control needs 2.21x as many tokens). At 50B content tokens, dQwen3.5-2B beats its full-attention control on 3/7 downstream benchmarks (control wins on math); dQwen3.5-9B beats Dream-7B (580B tokens), Dream-Coder-7B (322B tokens), and LLaDA-8B (2.3T tokens) on 4/7 benchmarks despite far less training. **Explicitly confirmed absence**: the paper contains **no per-layer ablation** isolating how much of the diffusion benefit comes from which specific attention layers being bidirectionalized, nor any sweep of *how many* attention layers need to flip — it flips all attention layers uniformly and compares only at the whole-model level against a full-attention baseline. This is exactly the gap our experiment targets, one level down (within the already-bidirectional ModernBERT, not within a hybrid AR backbone) — see Delta.

**LLM2Vec: Large Language Models Are Secretly Powerful Text Encoders**
BehnamGhader, Adlakha, Mosbach, Bahdanau, Hosseini, Reddy (arXiv:2404.05961).
Establishes the base recipe our thread 3 is compared against: (1) flip the causal mask to bidirectional (all-ones) **everywhere, uniformly**, (2) add masked-next-token-prediction (MNTP) training so the model adapts to seeing right context, (3) unsupervised contrastive learning (SimCSE-style, two dropout-masked passes). No layer-selective variant is tested in the base paper — it is a uniform full-bidirectionalization baseline, useful for our experiment as the "how much of this recipe's benefit could have come from bidirectionalizing far fewer layers" comparison point. UNVERIFIED: did not find a follow-up LLM2Vec ablation study.

**Causal2Vec: Improving Decoder-only LLMs as Versatile Embedding Models**
arXiv:2507.23386 (2026 revision cited; original earlier).
Takes the opposite tack from LLM2Vec: instead of flipping any layer's mask, it **prepends a single contextual token** (produced by a small BiLSTM/lightweight bidirectional pre-encoder) that carries a compressed bidirectional summary, and keeps the decoder-only LLM's own layers fully causal throughout. This is relevant as a contrasting design point: it achieves bidirectional-like performance benefits with **zero layers made bidirectional** in the main model, using position-level (prepended-token) injection instead of layer-level masking changes — a third point on the layer-vs-position tradeoff axis our experiment is mapping. UNVERIFIED: did not retrieve numeric benchmark deltas from this pass (search summary only, not deep-fetched).

**Training Hybrid Block Diffusion Language Models with Partial Bidirectionality**
Chaturvedi et al. (2026), arXiv:2607.02805.
The one paper found using the literal phrase "partial bidirectionality," but on inspection its "partial" refers to a different axis than layers: it's a **Mamba–attention hybrid block-diffusion model (BDLM Mamba-H)** where "partial bidirectionality" means the reverse (bidirectional) Mamba scan is **restricted to the currently-active denoising block** rather than the whole sequence (to preserve prefix-cacheability across blocks), not a restriction to specific layers or anchor positions. Numbers: at 87M params (DCLM sweep) BDLM Mamba-H gets the best C4-en validation perplexity among BDLM-Mamba/BDLM-attention/full-sequence baselines; stays competitive with BDLM-attention at 350M params; achieves **19.7x throughput** vs full-sequence DiffuMamba-H at 65K-token context and **3.7x** vs BDLM-attention at 262K context. Useful negative result for scoping: "partial bidirectionality" is an overloaded term in this literature — cite carefully, our sense (few layers / few positions) is distinct from this paper's sense (few positions in the sequence, applied uniformly across all bidirectionalized layers).

**Dream 7B / DiffuLLaMA (AR→diffusion initialization)**
Dream 7B: Ye, Xie et al. (HKU NLP, arXiv:2508.15487). DiffuLLaMA: cited as Dream 7B's direct predecessor/basis.
DiffuLLaMA adapts a causal LLaMA2-7B backbone into a diffusion LM by modifying the attention mechanism and mitigating causal-masking bias inherited from AR pretraining; Dream 7B extends this, initializing from Qwen2.5-7B weights and adding context-adaptive token-level noise rescheduling. Both are full-bidirectionalization approaches (all layers made bidirectional, unlike dQwen3.5's hybrid-preserving approach) — UNVERIFIED whether either paper reports any layer-selective ablation; search and fetch this pass surfaced only the headline architecture/initialization description, not a layer-count ablation. Training scale: Dream-7B is reported (via the Attention-Sinks-in-DLMs paper's comparison table) as trained on **580B tokens**, DiffuLLaMA on **322B** (as "Dream-Coder-7B" in that same comparison — verify variant naming), LLaDA-8B on **2.3T tokens** — all far larger than dQwen3.5's 50–100B-token adaptation budget, consistent with the thesis that hybrid/selective bidirectionalization is the cheap-adaptation path.

**Mercury / Gemini Diffusion**
Inception Labs' Mercury (Feb 2025) and Google DeepMind's Gemini Diffusion (May 2025) are both full-bidirectional-attention diffusion LMs per public description ("every position can attend to every other position ... essential for the denoising process"). No public technical report was retrieved this pass with layer-selective bidirectionality details for either — treat architecture specifics as UNVERIFIED beyond "full bidirectional attention, no causal masking reported."

## 4. ModernBERT architecture facts (verified against HF config + HF transformers source)

- **Layer count**: `num_hidden_layers = 22` for ModernBERT-base (confirmed from `answerdotai/ModernBERT-base/config.json`). ModernBERT-large is a separate, deeper checkpoint (UNVERIFIED exact count this pass, commonly cited as 28 — not confirmed against config in this survey).
- **Global/local alternation**: `global_attn_every_n_layers = 3` — i.e., **every 3rd layer (layer indices 0, 3, 6, …) is global attention**; all other layers use **local sliding-window attention with window size 128** (`local_attention = 128`, confirmed from config). Source: HF blog "Finally, a Replacement for BERT: Introducing ModernBERT," ACL 2025 paper (arXiv:2412.13663, "Smarter, Better, Faster, Longer"), and config.json.
- **RoPE theta split**: `global_rope_theta = 160000.0`, `local_rope_theta = 10000.0` (confirmed from config.json). Training note from the paper: base model trained at 1024 sequence length with RoPE theta 10,000 throughout, then context extended to 8192 by raising the **global**-layer theta to 160,000 for an additional ~300B tokens of training — i.e., the theta split is itself the product of a two-phase training schedule, not an architectural constant present from step 1. Relevant caveat for the experiment: global layers and local layers differ not just in mask pattern but in RoPE frequency, so a "global-attn-layer causally masked" ablation is not a clean isolation of "bidirectional vs causal" — it also changes what positional-frequency regime that layer operates in relative to a plain local layer.
- **HF `transformers` attention-mask handling**: confirmed by reading `modeling_modernbert.py` — the model **does accept a custom attention mask** in `forward()`, and internally builds **two separate mask objects**, `full_attention` (global, via `create_bidirectional_mask`) and `sliding_attention` (via `create_bidirectional_sliding_window_mask`), keyed by each layer's `layer_types[layer_idx]` entry (`"full_attention"` or `"sliding_attention"`). This means: (a) a straightforward **2D padding mask** is expanded automatically into both mask variants; (b) supplying a **custom 4D mask** is architecturally supported by the mask-mapping mechanism used elsewhere in `transformers` (per `modeling_attn_mask_utils.py`'s 4D-mask utilities and the `AttentionMaskConverter`), but ModernBERT's own helper functions (`create_bidirectional_mask`, `create_bidirectional_sliding_window_mask`) are the ones actually invoked per layer type — so a per-layer causal override for our experiment should be implemented by building a **custom callable/mask per layer** (or monkeypatching the mask dict passed to each `ModernBertEncoderLayer`) rather than assuming a single global `attention_mask` argument controls all layers uniformly. A `FlexAttention` code path also exists (PR huggingface/transformers#35423) that combines sliding-window + document masking via `torch.nn.attention.flex_attention`, which may be the more surgical route for per-layer/per-position custom masks than the eager 4D-mask path. UNVERIFIED: whether the currently-released `transformers` version's eager/sdpa attention path will accept a *fully custom* 4D mask that overrides the sliding-window structure per layer without further code changes — treat as needing a direct code test before committing to method (a) of the experiment.
- Additional documented facts (not independently re-verified this pass but standard/uncontested in the ACL paper and HF blog): unpadding (removing padded tokens before compute), GeGLU activations, trained on 2T tokens, native 8192 context after the RoPE-theta extension phase, Flash Attention 3 used for global layers / Flash Attention 2 for local layers where available.

## 5. "Bidirectional info is redundant in most layers" / causal-BERT / few-layer sufficiency results

- **DecBERT** (already detailed in §1) is the most directly on-point published result: causal masking of a *small prefix of layers* (2 of presumably 12) is not just tolerable but can *substitute for* position embeddings and modestly help GLUE — i.e., existing evidence that an encoder's bidirectional benefit is **not uniformly distributed across layers**, consistent with our hypothesis, though DecBERT never frames or measures it as "redundancy of bidirectionality" — it frames it as "causal masks are a free positional signal," and never sweeps single-layer causal ablation or measures MLM-loss degradation directly.
- **MosaicBERT / NomicBERT**: both are architecture-and-training-efficiency papers (FlashAttention, ALiBi, GLU, unpadding for Mosaic; SwiGLU + RoPE for Nomic). Neither was found (via search + fetch this pass) to run a causal-vs-bidirectional ablation; they simply build faster bidirectional encoders. Confirmed absent, not merely unfetched: search summaries explicitly note "did not contain specific details about causal vs. bidirectional attention ablations." UNVERIFIED beyond that.
- **UniLM-style prefix-LM training** (background, not deep-fetched this pass): trains one Transformer stack under interchangeable masks (bidirectional / L2R / seq2seq) chosen per training example, and different downstream tasks are fine-tuned with different masks — again a *training-time, per-example* mask choice, not a *per-layer, single-forward-pass* architecture choice, so does not directly answer "which layers carry the bidirectional signal."
- No source located this pass runs the specific "unidirectional MLM" ablation (train/eval an otherwise-BERT-shaped model with a strictly left-to-right mask on all layers and quantify the GLUE/perplexity gap) with a clean number beyond the general folklore statement (repeated in search summaries, sourced ultimately to the BERT paper's own left-to-right ablation) that GPT-style full causal masking is "significantly lower... across all tasks" — the *specific numeric gap* was not retrieved as a citable figure this pass. UNVERIFIED as a number; treat as qualitative-only.

## Delta: what nobody has measured, and design pitfalls flagged by sources

- **Nobody runs the single-layer causal-mask sweep.** DecBERT tests one hand-picked 2-layer prefix; no source found does "causally mask layer *i* alone (for each *i* in turn) and measure ΔMLM-loss" on any BERT-style encoder, let alone ModernBERT specifically with its global/local alternation. This is the core novel measurement in design (a).
- **Nobody has run a layer ablation on ModernBERT's own global/local structure specifically.** Because global and local layers already differ in mask pattern, window size, *and* RoPE theta, our per-layer causal-mask experiment will confound "bidirectional→causal" with "which RoPE-theta regime that layer runs in" unless controlled for — no source addresses this because ModernBERT itself is new enough that no bidirectionality-probe paper targets it.
- **dQwen3.5 bidirectionalizes 100% of its attention layers uniformly and never tests whether fewer would suffice** — explicitly confirmed no per-layer ablation exists in that paper. Our experiment answers, one level down, the question dQwen3.5's own authors left open at the attention-layer-selection level.
- **The DLM attention-sink paper (2510.15731) gives the strongest quantitative precedent that anchor-restricted attention is cheap in bidirectional/diffusion models** (masking 5 sinks costs 1–3%, 10 sinks up to ~17% worst case) but does so in *already-fully-bidirectional* 7–8B diffusion LMs, not by comparing against a genuinely causal-masked baseline, and not on an *encoder* (ModernBERT) with an MLM objective — our design (b)/(c) would be the first to connect that anchor-recoverability result to an actual causal-vs-bidirectional loss-degradation baseline on an encoder.
- **Pitfall flagged directly by a source**: the DLM attention-sink paper found sink *positions shift dynamically* across the denoising/generation process (rightward, right-to-left, or static depending on model) — a fixed anchor set (e.g., always CLS/SEP/punctuation) risks missing the actual sinks if ModernBERT's future-attending mass is similarly non-stationary across sequence position or masking pattern; the experiment should check anchor stability across many masked positions before committing to a fixed anchor set for design (b).
- **Pitfall flagged directly by a source**: StreamingLLM's sink tokens are positionally defined (first ~4 tokens by position, regardless of content) while BERT's anchors (Clark et al.) are content/type-defined ([CLS]/[SEP]/punctuation). If ModernBERT's future-attention mass turns out to be positional (e.g., concentrated on the first global-attention token slot) rather than content-driven, an anchor set built from punctuation/[SEP] heuristics (as in design (b)) could underperform a purely positional anchor set — worth testing both.
- **No source varies bidirectionality by architecture-mixing type the way ModernBERT would require**: dQwen3.5's layer split is attention-vs-RNN (structural); ours would be global-vs-local *within a pure-attention* stack — a genuinely different and unaddressed axis, since local (sliding-window) layers are already partially "local-bidirectional" and causally masking them changes a smaller effective window than causally masking a global layer.
- **Causal2Vec's "prepend one bidirectional summary token, keep all decoder layers causal" result** is an unexamined third option: rather than making any of ModernBERT's own layers causal, one could ask the inverse question (how much of ModernBERT's bidirectional MLM performance survives if right-context is only visible as one compressed summary token) — nobody has run this on an encoder MLM objective, and it would be a natural complementary ablation to (b)/(c) rather than a replacement.
