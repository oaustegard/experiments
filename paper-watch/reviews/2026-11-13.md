## HIGH

- **27. e-means: outperforming k-means (in one-tenth the epochs)**, [2611.09501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09501) (166 upvotes, read: full). **Prior work: already adopted.**
  - [`emeans-residual/`](../emeans-residual/RESULTS.md) (2026-11-10): the claim reproduces on 100k EmbeddingGemma 2 vectors (1.6–10.2× sooner on 4 CPUs). Centroid-residual remex codes raise 1-bit R@10 from 0.840 to 0.891, but the centroid table costs more than a second bit at this corpus size.
  - [`emeans-pooled-residual/`](../emeans-pooled-residual/RESULTS.md) (2026-11-13): pooled LateOn tokens gain +0.010 to +0.021 nDCG@10 at 1 bit; a 61-job grid maps where e-means pays off.
  - [`emeans-explainer/`](../emeans-explainer/README.md) (2026-11-13): narrated video.
  - Blog post: [Two passes of e-means](https://muninn.austegard.com/blog/two-passes-of-e-means.html) (Muninn memory `1a81a162`).
  - Muninn trigger procedure `ad60e592` holds the measured decision rules (d ≥ 32 and n/k ≥ ~50: e-means; n/k < ~25: Lloyd; d = 3 in faiss: k ≤ 64 only).
  - The paper's own numbers check out (Table 4: 1.4–2.6 epochs to match Flash-KMeans@25, 9.5–45.5× GPU wall-clock; code in faiss `contrib/e_means`). Colour quantization (d = 3) is the measured exception: in faiss, e-means is slower than Lloyd from k ≈ 256 (0.1–0.47× from k = 1,024), and faster only at palette sizes k ≤ 64 (1.3–3.3×). RESULTS.md finding 8 puts the loss down to faiss's cheap full pass and costly small-batch assignment, and leaves open whether a hand-written JS/WGSL loop avoids it.
