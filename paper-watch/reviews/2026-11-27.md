## MEDIUM

- [Recursive Collective Intelligence: Emergence on an Equal-Budget Ladder in Multi-Agent LLM Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32606) (rank 30, 159 upvotes, read: full). Grades multi-agent groups against a compute-matched single agent, a majority vote and pass@n. Debate protocols do not beat the vote on shared-information tasks. Forwarding raw split facts does: at 27B, four agents each holding part of the facts score 0.959 against 0.942 for an equal-budget full-information agent, +0.31 over pass@n (Table 2, checked against the full text). Debate fails because its messages carry conclusions drawn from partial facts, and readers adopt those conclusions. For subagent delegation, a subagent earns its tokens only when it holds private context, and its handback should forward evidence rather than conclusions. Compute-matched voting is the baseline to beat. This agrees in direction with Kim et al. 2512.08296 but does not test its ~45% single-agent threshold. The recursive self-training results need verifiable tasks and a training loop. Code: not linked.

## LOW

- [Mixture-of-Loops Transformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34923) (rank 29, 165 upvotes, read: full). A looped transformer whose later loops attend over earlier loops' keys and values (MoLA) instead of reading a gated residual. The 1.7x token-efficiency claim against OLMo3 at 700M costs about 2.0x training FLOPs and 2.0x wall-clock (Table 11), and the paper runs no FLOP-matched comparison. Continued pretraining of Qwen3.5-0.8B on 100B tokens gains 0.016 nats of CE (2.480 vs 2.496). It offers no cheap retrofit, so it leaves the monad-looped negative result standing. If a loop retrofit is tried again, one idea is worth testing: mix the loop sources by softmax attention with no separate gate, since the gate is what collapsed to ~0.01 in monad-looped. Code: hustvl/MoLT announced, not yet released according to the body.
