Read a page with Tesseract, on your own GPU

Drop a scan or a photo of a page. Indra OCR finds the text lines and their reading order; Tesseract's LSTM recognizer, run as WebGPU kernels, reads each line in any of the 320 LSTM models in tessdata_fast and tessdata_best (about 160 languages and scripts, each in both sizes; the legacy-only equ and osd models are not offered). The page is not uploaded anywhere. Models come from Datalab's and Tesseract's public repositories: the first use of a language downloads its model. It needs a browser with WebGPU; without one the page shows a message and does nothing else.

fast: Google's 8-bit model, about 1 to 5 MB a language. best: the float model, about 1.5 to 15 MB, slightly more accurate.

Page

Text, in reading order

    How it works: Indra's detector finds the text lines and its small transformer puts them in reading order. Indra also reads each line with its own recognizer; that text is only shown for comparison. Each line is cropped from the page and read on the GPU by Tesseract's LSTM recognizer, the same network Tesseract 5 runs on the CPU, ported to WebGPU kernels. Math lines are found by Indra's math classifier, which flags a line, and Indra's math reader writes it as LaTeX; Tesseract has no math model, so flagged lines are not sent to it. Language models are Tesseract's tessdata_fast (about 1 to 5 MB per language) and tessdata_best (about 1.5 to 15 MB, slightly more accurate), loaded from jsDelivr on first use.

    Downloads: Indra's layout models, about 4 MB, fetched once; its math reader, 4 to 8 MB, only when a math line appears; plus the Tesseract model for the language you pick.
