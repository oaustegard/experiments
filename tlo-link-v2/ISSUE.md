Webex broke a deck link. It percent-encoded every `~` twice (`%257E`), and `parseHash` doesn't percent-decode the payload, so the deck opened empty. Webex also pulled the closing `’` that followed the link into it. That led to a look at the payload format.

The write-up, code and data are in [oaustegard/experiments/tlo-link-v2](https://github.com/oaustegard/experiments/blob/main/tlo-link-v2/RESULTS.md). The numbers are measured on your own documents. The 35 files in `samples/` were held out. Dictionaries were trained on the 193 documents in `test/doc/corpus`.

- **A 32 KB deflate dictionary under a new flag makes links 17.6% shorter, with fflate as it is.**
  - zstd's COVER trainer picked the dictionary content, and v1's dictionary sits at the end.
  - Smaller dictionaries cost less: 8 KB gives −11.6% for +2.8 KB gzipped, and 16 KB gives −14.5% for +6.0 KB. 32 KB gives −18.3% for +13.2 KB.
  - Documents unlike the corpus shrink 2–9%.
  - zstd with the same dictionary size is only 2.5 points better, and brotli at quality 11 is 0.5% better than v1.
- **A base62 body costs 0.78% and drops `~`, `-` and `_` from links.** Each of the three has a known failure:
  - Webex's double encoding of `~`;
  - GFM's autolinker dropping a trailing `_` or `~`;
  - the line-break opportunity after `-`, which is why `codec.ts` uses `~`.
- **A reader that percent-decodes until stable and keeps only alphanumerics decoded all 229 corpus links correctly, in Python and in Node.** The test manglings were double percent-encoding, 76-column mail wrapping, a zero-width space and a glued quote.
- **An uppercase base32 body for QR codes makes the codes smaller.** In QR alphanumeric mode, a 2 KB deck goes from version 26 to version 23, even though its text is 19% longer.
- **One link in 229, with its last character cut, decoded to the wrong document without error.** This happens in v1 as in v2, because raw deflate has no checksum. A CRC-16 in 3 characters would catch it. It is listed as optional, and I haven't measured it.

The proposed shape is `#/v2/<app>/<flag><body>`:

- flags `w`/`y`/`z` with base62 bodies;
- the same flags in uppercase (`W`/`Y`/`Z`) with base32 bodies, for QR;
- `r` dropped;
- v1 links still read.

[`codec_v2.mjs`](https://github.com/oaustegard/experiments/blob/main/tlo-link-v2/codec_v2.mjs) has the base62 block codec and the forgiving reader in the shape `codec.ts` could take. It uses BigInt and has no dependencies. [`codec_v2.py`](https://github.com/oaustegard/experiments/blob/main/tlo-link-v2/codec_v2.py) is the reference for the whole format.

Only the Webex behaviour was observed. The other platform failures come from specs and from the comments in `codec.ts`. The benchmark can rerun against a branch if that would help.
