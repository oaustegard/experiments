Images as Shaders

Each picture below is one WGSL fragment shader. The shader holds an ordered list of 1,024 soft, rotated ellipses packed into an integer array, and paints them per pixel; its minified source is about 11 KB gzipped. The list was trained so that its first 16, 32, 64 and so on ellipses already make a coarser picture. Press "Paint it in" to watch the shader add them, or drag the splats slider to stop at a power of two. As compression it loses: on these six Kodak test photos, AVIF at the same size scores 3.9 to 6.8 dB higher in PSNR. The buttons under the picture show the original and the AVIF and WebP files of the same size.

Your image stays in this browser. It is scaled down (512 px on a phone, 768 px otherwise, on its longer side), then fitted on your GPU with TensorFlow.js; the picture above updates while it trains. The splat menu lists what this device can train, with a time estimate from a quick test of its GPU. Phones stop at 256 splats, since larger lists ran out of memory on an iPhone; computers go up to 2,048.
