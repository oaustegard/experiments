# The Ilvari sky — world bible

The Ilvari are a cliff-dwelling people of a cold, windy northern archipelago
(latitude about 60° N). They fish, keep goats and seabirds, weave sailcloth, and
above all they fly kites: for weather, for courtship, for mourning, for asking
questions of the wind. Their word for the sky is **Vel**, "the Upper Sea".

## Cosmology

Long ago the sky lay close over the islands, tethered to the earth by a single
string held at the pole by **Mother Hesk**, the Reel. **Aun**, the First Flier,
wanted to know how far the wind went, so he cut the string. The sky drifted up
and away and has been far ever since. Every star is a lamp: either a kite whose
string was cut (the dead "go aloft" as kites), a lantern on a boat of the Upper
Sea, or a spark from Aun's knife. The people's long hope is to fly back.

## The pantheon (use these; do not invent new gods)

- **Aun** — the First Flier. Curious, reckless, beloved, guilty. Trickster-hero.
- **Hesk** — Mother Reel at the pole. Patient, winds every string, keeps count.
- **Tavo** — the Gale. Lifts and wrecks. Laughs. Cannot be bargained with.
- **Ennu** — the Kite-Wright. Builds kites, boats and bodies from cloth and bone.
- **Sil** — the Moon, the Drowned Lantern. Aun's sister, who still searches for him.
- **Orrun** — the Deep-Whale beneath both seas, who carries the islands on his back.
- **Kael** — the Netter, who casts for fallen stars and never catches one.
- **The Gulls** — a chorus of messengers between Vel and the islands.

## The five houses of the sky

- **The Spindle** (circling the north pole, never sets): hearth, the Reel,
  elders, household things, things that endure and keep count.
- **The Windward Reach** (northern sky): weather, birds, kite-makers, courtship,
  the living work of the islands.
- **The Lamp Road** (the band the sun and moon travel): the road the Sun-Kite is
  flown along through the year; seasons, labours, festivals, journeys.
- **The Leeward Reach** (southern sky, low from home): voyagers, storms, trade,
  the drowned, things far off and half-seen.
- **The Unflown** (deep south, never visible from the islands): known only from
  sailors' tales. Uncanny, rumoured, unreliable, beautiful. Things lost go here.

## Language (for every Ilvari word you coin)

- Consonants: k t s l r n m v h, and th, sk. No b d g p f z w y c j q x.
- Vowels: a e i o u; long vowels written á é í ó ú.
- Syllables CV or CVC. Names are 2–3 syllables, e.g. Hesk, Tavo, Ennu,
  Skelra, Ithúna, Varo, Mesket, Óllun.
- Format a name as `Ilvari word` with an English gloss: *Skelra* — "the Split Oar".

## Register

Spare, maritime, tender, a little uncanny. Concrete objects, weather, hands,
salt, rope, cloth, bone, smoke. Humour is dry. No high fantasy vocabulary (no
"realm", "ancient power", "destiny", "mystical", "ethereal", "tapestry").
Myths read like something a grandmother tells while mending a net, not a
fantasy novel blurb.
