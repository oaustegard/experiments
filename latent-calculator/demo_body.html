A frozen language model, a calculator, and no tokens between them. SmolLM2-135M never sees the tool: a small trained head reads the operands out of its layer-16 activations, a Python calculator does the arithmetic, and a small trained encoder writes the result back into the residual stream after layer 16, once per answer step. Every model weight is frozen. Nothing is appended to the prompt. The model then answers as if it knew.

Below: the same twelve prompts through the frozen model alone, through the usual text route (the result pasted into the prompt as tokens), and through the latent port. Then the numbers over 2,000 prompts.

How the port works
  • Asking. A cross-attention head with 13 learned queries reads the operator and twelve digit slots out of the layer-16 activations of every prompt token (232k parameters). It recovers the full query on 99% of in-distribution prompts. Reading one vector at the last token, the phase-1 design, managed 10%: the digits live at their own tokens, not at the question mark.
  • Computing. Plain Python on the decoded query. Exact.
  • Reading. An encoder turns the result (sign, digits, or a comparison word) plus the answer-step index into one vector of the model's hidden size and adds it to the residual stream after layer 16, at the query position and again at every answer step. The digits are stored most-significant first, so answer step j reads slot j. The frozen upper layers turn that into the next token. Trained by the model's own next-token loss; 91% exact with true operands.
  • Cost. Zero tokens in either direction. The port's own compute is a few matrix products per step; the wall-clock difference in the tables is mostly that the frozen model rambles for 16 tokens and the ported one stops after the answer.
Where it fails
  • Twelve-digit products. Multiplying two 6-digit numbers lands at 66%; the wrong answers have the leading digits right and the last few off. Addition and subtraction, whose results stay under eight digits, are at 99% and 98%.
  • Lengths it never saw. On prompts with a 5-digit operand the reading side holds up (85% with true operands) but the query head is right only 45% of the time, so the whole pipeline lands at 41%. The head counts digit positions from the end of the prompt, and an unseen operand length shifts the count.
  • The text route scores zero on exact match here because a 135M base model wraps the pasted result in prose or repeats it; the "contains" column is its fair number.

SmolLM2-135M, frozen, fp32, 4 CPU cores. Trained parts: query head 232k, result encoder about 1M. Port at layer 16 of 30, chosen by a linear-probe sweep. 20,000 synthetic training prompts over twelve templates. Code, predictions and results: oaustegard/experiments/latent-calculator.
