Turn a page into markdown, on your own GPU

Drop a scan, a photo of a page or a PDF. A small OCR model finds the text lines, reads them and puts them in reading order, with headings, lists and tables, all inside this tab: the page is not uploaded anywhere. It is Indra OCR by Datalab, its weights stored as 16-bit floats, which score the same as the originals: the first visit downloads about 4 MB. It needs a browser with WebGPU; it was tested in Chrome on a Mac.

How it works: a detector finds the text lines. A recognizer reads each line; the first one reads Latin, Greek, Cyrillic,
  Devanagari and Bengali, and three more are downloaded (2 to 9 MB each, with their word models) the first time a line needs them: Chinese,
  Japanese and Korean; Arabic and Hebrew; and other scripts such as Thai, Ethiopic and the South Indian scripts. A line the
  recognizer is unsure of is read again with a word model (an n-gram table of which characters usually follow which) that
  breaks close calls; the Latin one (2.5 MB) is downloaded the first time a line needs it. A small transformer then puts the lines in reading order and labels headings, lists and tables.
  Everything runs on WebGPU, in code ported from Datalab's indra-ocr
  Python package: given the same weights, the two produce the same text and markdown on the pages compared.
  Write-up: indra-browser.
