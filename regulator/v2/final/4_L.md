*Interview with Mademoiselle Lina Ruedi, régleuse, in the dining room. Fourth interview of the night.*

MATTHEY: Please sit down, Mademoiselle. I will ask short questions. Take your time.

MLLE RUEDI: I would rather be asked than wait. Ask.

MATTHEY: Begin with dinner. Who was at the table?

MLLE RUEDI: The family, Dr Vauthier, Monsieur Girard, and myself at the end, because I am the hand from the atelier and the master liked me where he could see me.

MATTHEY: Was anything done at dinner that was unusual?

MLLE RUEDI: Not for the master. When the temple struck nine, he made us all take out our watches. He does it every year, and he tells each owner what is wrong with it. Mine was exact. I had regulated it myself, and he knew it. The doctor's was two minutes ahead. Monsieur Girard would not take his out of his pocket. Monsieur Félix would not let anyone touch his late father's silver watch, and he would not let the master touch it either.

MATTHEY: After dinner, where did you go?

MLLE RUEDI: Up to the atelier. I worked beside the master. He had me checking a hairspring for the repeater.

MATTHEY: Tell me about the repeater. I confess I do not understand how it works.

MLLE RUEDI: It has been four years of his life. When you press the slide, it sounds the time. Low notes for the hours, a doubled note for each quarter, and high notes for each minute past the quarter. He wanted it to strike the first minute of the new century, and he would not hurry a single part of it. He said exactness is only a habit that has not yet been tested.

MATTHEY: When the temple struck ten, what was the regulator doing?

MLLE RUEDI: It agreed with the bell exactly. I looked at it.

MATTHEY: And after that?

MLLE RUEDI: He sent me downstairs. The regulator showed twenty past ten. It was running then, and he was well. He said I looked like a woman who had not eaten, and that I was no use to him with that face. I went down.

MATTHEY: Did you go straight to the kitchen?

MLLE RUEDI: Straight down to the kitchen. I stayed there with Rosa from when I came down until after the temple struck eleven.

MATTHEY: What did you do in the kitchen?

MLLE RUEDI: I compared my watch with Rosa's kitchen clock. Her kitchen clock was exactly fifteen minutes fast. She keeps it that way on purpose, so that nothing in the house is ever late. I said nothing to her about it.

MATTHEY: Did you hear anything from the upper floors?

MLLE RUEDI: From the kitchen we heard Madame's piano. It began at twelve minutes to eleven by the kitchen clock, and it stopped at one minute past eleven by the kitchen clock. I was in the kitchen.

MATTHEY: Thank you. You have been very exact.

MLLE RUEDI: It is the only way I know how to speak about the work. I will not guess at who would want to harm him. He taught me to count the beats in my head so I would not need to look at a watch.

MATTHEY: Is there anything you have not told me that you think I should know?

MLLE RUEDI: Only that he was well at twenty past ten. I want that written down.
