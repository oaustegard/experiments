*Interview with Monsieur Bastien Girard, établisseur of Le Locle, in the dining room. Sixth interview of the night.*

MATTHEY: Monsieur Girard, please sit down. The stove in here is poor and you are cold. I will not keep you long. Tell me what you did this evening, in your own order.

GIRARD: I have already told the gendarme what I know, Inspector, and I will say it again if you like. I came to dinner because Monnier asked me, and because I wanted the repeater. I offered him a fair price for the rights to the movement and he laughed at me across the table. We quarrelled about the price. Half the table heard it.

MATTHEY: Yes. What did you do after dinner?

GIRARD: I sat on alone in the dining room with the brandy. At dinner, when the temple struck nine, Monnier had made us all show our watches, as he does every year. I kept mine in my pocket. A merchant's watch is his own affair, I told him, and he said my hands were a disgrace at a watchmaker's table. He was right about the hands. I have no patience with men who are always right.

MATTHEY: Were you still there when the temple struck ten?

GIRARD: I was. I heard it over the brandy and did not trouble to look at my watch. I had had enough of watches for one evening.

MATTHEY: And then?

GIRARD: I wanted air and a cigar. I smoke Brissago, the cheap ones, and I will not apologise for them. I went out into the street at twenty-one minutes past ten by my watch and I stayed under the front windows of the house. It was bitter cold. The snow in the street has been there a week and is hard as iron. I like the cold when I want to think, and I stood there thinking about the repeater.

MATTHEY: Did you hear anything from the house?

GIRARD: The piano started up in the salon just as I lit my cigar, at twenty-nine minutes past ten by my watch. I remember it because I was thinking that a man should be listening to his guests, not playing for them.

MATTHEY: Who was at the piano?

GIRARD: I cannot say. I was in the street, and I will not put a name to a sound I did not see.

MATTHEY: Did you hear anything else?

GIRARD: At twenty-six minutes to eleven by my watch I heard faint quick bells from the master's window up above, and the piano was playing all the while. What did I take them for? A repeater, I would have said. In my trade you know the sound. But I was in the street and I will not swear to more than I heard.

MATTHEY: And when did the piano stop?

GIRARD: It stopped at eighteen minutes to eleven by my watch.

MATTHEY: You came back into the house.

GIRARD: I came back in at fourteen minutes to eleven by my watch. The hall clock had just struck eleven, and its face showed two minutes past eleven. I have set down what I saw on it, Inspector, and I have no opinion of it to add.

MATTHEY: And then you went upstairs.

GIRARD: I went up to Félix in the library.

MATTHEY: You knew Félix was in the library?

GIRARD: He owes me money. A sum I will not name in front of his aunt. I am not ashamed to say I wanted to see him about it. That is no crime in Le Locle or anywhere else.

MATTHEY: Had you any dealings with Dr Vauthier this evening?

GIRARD: I don't care for the doctor's company and he does not care for mine. He talks Latin at a man over the cards and watches to see if you understand him. I did not play cards with him tonight and I was glad of it.

MATTHEY: Monsieur Girard, is there anything you have not told me that you think I should know?

GIRARD: Only that I was angry with Monnier about the price, and he knew it. I have been angry with men before and I have never killed one. Write that down if you must.
