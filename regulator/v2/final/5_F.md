*Interview with Monsieur Félix Monnier, nephew of the deceased, in the dining room. Fifth interview of the night.*

MATTHEY: Sit down, Monsieur Monnier. You are the nephew.

FÉLIX: I am. Jules's boy. My father was Aurèle's younger brother, if you want the family tree. I can draw it on the tablecloth, though my aunt would not thank me.

MATTHEY: Tell me about dinner.

FÉLIX: It was the usual thing. Uncle holds his inspection every year. When the temple struck nine we all had to put our watches on the cloth, and he went round the table telling each of us what was wrong with ours. Very amusing, for him.

MATTHEY: And yours?

FÉLIX: Mine is my father's, a silver watch, very old. When the temple struck nine, my watch showed twenty minutes to nine. I said something about it, I suppose too loudly, and my uncle made a scene about it in front of everyone. He said it was a mausoleum, or near enough. Girard laughed. Odile looked at her plate. I was sorry I had ever taken it out.

MATTHEY: Has anyone touched it?

FÉLIX: No. It has given me trouble for ten years and I have never let anyone near it, my uncle included. He would have liked to. He always wants to fix things that aren't his.

MATTHEY: What did you do after dinner?

FÉLIX: I went up to the library. I had a glass of brandy, then another. I like the library. It is quiet and the books don't tell you what is wrong with you. I stayed there alone.

MATTHEY: Did you look at the time?

FÉLIX: I heard the temple strike ten. I did not look at the watch. I did not want to know what it would say.

MATTHEY: Tell me what you heard after that.

FÉLIX: At a quarter past ten by my watch someone went quickly up the stairs, past the library door. I did not see who. The door was shut. I thought nothing of it then.

MATTHEY: Go on.

FÉLIX: At eighteen minutes past ten by my watch I heard a thump on the floor right above me. In the atelier. Then my uncle's repeater. I know that sound. He had been four years on that watch and he played it for anyone who would sit still. Ten slow deep notes. I counted those. Then some doubled notes, then quick high ones. I did not count those. My aunt's piano was playing downstairs when the thump came. I could hear it through the floor, Chopin, I think. She plays it very well.

MATTHEY: And after the thump?

FÉLIX: At twenty-one minutes past ten by my watch someone came quickly down the stairs. Again I did not see who. The door was shut.

MATTHEY: What did you think the thump was?

FÉLIX: I thought he had dropped something heavy. That is what I told myself. I was alone in the library, Inspector, directly under his bench. Do you see how that looks?

MATTHEY: I am asking what you did.

FÉLIX: I stayed where I was. I finished the brandy.

MATTHEY: Did anyone come to the library?

FÉLIX: Bastien Girard came into the library at thirty-two minutes past ten by my watch. Girard said he wanted a word, and then he sat down and we sat together there until the temple struck eleven. He was not in a good humour. Neither was I. We talked about money, mainly mine.

MATTHEY: What money?

FÉLIX: I owe him some. It is not a secret in this town. I owe a few other people too. My uncle knew, and he did not like it, and he said so at dinner.

MATTHEY: Did you know what the repeater meant to him?

FÉLIX: Everything. Vauthier used to say my uncle loved that watch more than his wife, and Odile would say that was not a difficult thing to do. Forgive me. I talk when I am frightened. I am very frightened, Inspector.

MATTHEY: Thank you, Monsieur Monnier. That is all for now.
