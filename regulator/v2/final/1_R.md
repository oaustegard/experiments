*Interview with Rosa Häfeli, maid and cook, in the dining room. First interview of the night.*

MATTHEY: Sit down, Madame Häfeli. Take your time. You are not in any trouble with me.

ROSA: Ja, Herr Inspektor. Herrgott, I cannot think. I have not sat down once tonight, and now I am sitting in the dining room with the police. The master is lying up there. I still cannot believe it.

MATTHEY: I know. I am sorry to bring you back to the house. Let us go slowly. Tell me about the evening, from the beginning, when you were in the kitchen.

ROSA: The kitchen is at the back, you know it. The glasses for the toast were on the table, all washed, and I dried them myself with the good cloth. Madame wanted the champagne cold, so it was in the cold box with the ice. Everything was ready. I do not like to be late with anything, Herr Inspektor. That is why I keep my clock fast.

MATTHEY: Your clock is fast?

ROSA: I keep it fast on purpose, so that nothing in my kitchen is ever late. The dinner must go out when the bell says, and Madame is exact. She says I am a tyrant about my clock.

MATTHEY: Good. Now the bell. Can you tell me what your clock showed when the temple struck ten?

ROSA: When the temple struck ten, my kitchen clock showed a quarter past ten. I keep it fast on purpose.

MATTHEY: Thank you. Did anyone come to the kitchen?

ROSA: Fräulein Lina came down into the kitchen at twenty-three minutes to eleven by my kitchen clock, and she stayed with me until after the temple struck eleven. She did not say much. She is a quiet girl.

MATTHEY: Did you hear anything from upstairs, or from the front of the house, while Fräulein Lina was with you?

ROSA: We heard Madame's piano begin at twelve minutes to eleven by my kitchen clock, and stop at one minute past eleven by my kitchen clock. Madame plays Chopin. Those are sad pieces, Herr Inspektor. When she stopped, the kitchen was very quiet.

MATTHEY: And the hall clock?

ROSA: I heard the hall clock strike eleven when my kitchen clock showed three minutes past eleven. The old Morbier, in the hall. You hear it all through the house.

MATTHEY: Thank you, Madame Häfeli. You have been very clear. Now I must ask about the other part of the night, when you went up for the master.

ROSA: Ja. I went up to fetch him for the toast. The toast was ready and the glasses were on the tray, and I said to myself, the master will want to see the new year come in with his watch in his hand, the way he does. So I went up the stairs to the atelier, and I opened the door, and he was on the floor. Herrgott, the regulator on the wall had stopped. And he had his repeater in his hand. I did not touch him.

MATTHEY: Did you touch anything else in the atelier?

ROSA: No, Herr Inspektor. I know what the police do.

MATTHEY: You are not afraid of me, I hope.

ROSA: I am afraid of the police, Herr Inspektor, every woman in my position is. But I am not afraid of telling the truth. I have been twenty years in service in this town. I was born in the Emmental and I know my place. Madame has been good to me. She is a hard woman, but she is fair, and she would never want me to lie for her or for anyone. So I tell you what I know.

MATTHEY: That is all I ask. Is there anything you have not told me, however small, that you think I should know?

ROSA: Only that the champagne is still in the cold box. We were going to drink to the new century. Herrgott, the new century.

MATTHEY: Thank you, Madame Häfeli. Please wait in the kitchen. I may need you again.

ROSA: Ja, Herr Inspektor.
