# The Regulator

*La Chaux-de-Fonds, the night of 31 December 1899*

## The inspector's note

I was called to the house of Aurèle Monnier, master watchmaker, Rue du Progrès, shortly
after the bells had rung in the new century. Monnier was dead on the floor of his
atelier, struck once from behind with the brass mallet from his own bench. His maid,
Rosa Häfeli, had found him a little before midnight, when she went up to fetch him for
the toast.

The house has three floors. At the top is the atelier, with the master's bench under the
long windows and the precision regulator on the wall. The atelier's pine floor is the
library's ceiling. On the ground floor are the hall, where the stairs come down and an old
Morbier long-case clock stands; the salon at the front, with the piano and a marble
mantel clock; the dining room; and the kitchen at the back. The salon and atelier windows
look over the street.

Six people were in the house that evening besides the dead man: his wife Odile; his
nephew Félix; Lina Ruedi, the régleuse from his atelier; Dr Casimir Vauthier, physician
and friend of the family; Bastien Girard, an établisseur from Le Locle; and Rosa.

I found these things in the atelier.

- **The regulator had stopped.** Its hands stood at five past ten and its pendulum hung
  still.
- **Monnier's new repeater was in his right hand, its slide pressed home.** A minute
  repeater sounds the time when the slide is pressed: one low note for each hour, one
  doubled note for each quarter past the hour, and one high note for each minute past
  the quarter. He had pressed it as he fell.

I interviewed all six, one at a time, in the dining room, between one and four in the
morning. My clerk's transcripts follow, in the order I took them.

## What I hold to be true

1. One of the six struck the blow. Whoever it was stood in the atelier when the repeater
   sounded, because the repeater sounded at the moment of the blow.
2. The murderer has lied to me. The other five have told me honestly what they saw and
   heard. What any of them believes about a clock is another matter.
3. Every clock and watch in this house keeps its own time, and most of them disagree. None was
   adjusted between dinner and midnight except by the murderer's hand. When an honest
   witness tells me what a timepiece showed, that is what it showed.
4. The bell of the Grand Temple strikes the true hour.
5. Nobody in this house needs more than five minutes to go from any room to any other.

## The question

Who struck Aurèle Monnier, and at what true time, to the minute?
