*Interview with Mademoiselle Lina Ruedi, régleuse, in the dining room. Fourth interview of the night.*

MATTHEY: Mademoiselle, thank you for waiting. Please tell me about the evening in order, and take your time.

MLLE RUEDI: At nine, when the temple struck, the master had us all show our watches. He does it every year. He tells each person what is wrong with theirs.

MATTHEY: And yours?

MLLE RUEDI: Mine was exact. I regulated it myself.

MATTHEY: Who else showed theirs?

MLLE RUEDI: Dr Vauthier's was two minutes ahead. Monsieur Girard would not take his out of his pocket.

MATTHEY: After dinner, where did you go?

MLLE RUEDI: Up to the atelier. I worked beside the master at his bench, as I always do.

MATTHEY: Tell me what you saw of the clocks while you were working.

MLLE RUEDI: The hall clock downstairs struck ten. At that moment the regulator showed twelve minutes to ten. The master said what he always says about that clock.

MATTHEY: What does he say?

MLLE RUEDI: It is an old remark between him and the clock. I will not repeat it, Inspector, it is not evidence.

MATTHEY: Understood. And when the temple struck ten?

MLLE RUEDI: The regulator agreed with the bell exactly.

MATTHEY: When did he send you downstairs?

MLLE RUEDI: When the regulator showed twenty past ten. The regulator was running then, and he was well. I went straight down to the kitchen.

MATTHEY: Had anything troubled him that evening?

MLLE RUEDI: Not that I saw. He was himself.

MATTHEY: Had you seen the master in the kitchen earlier in the day?

MLLE RUEDI: That afternoon, yes. I saw him in the kitchen with Rosa's American alarm clock in his hands. He was laughing.

MATTHEY: Do you know why he had it?

MLLE RUEDI: I did not ask him why.

MATTHEY: Let us return to the evening. Where were you when Madame began to play?

MLLE RUEDI: In the kitchen with Rosa. When the piano began, I looked at the kitchen clock, which is Rosa's American alarm clock. It showed two minutes to eleven.

MATTHEY: And when the piano stopped?

MLLE RUEDI: I looked again. The kitchen clock, Rosa's American alarm clock, showed eleven minutes past eleven.

MATTHEY: You are certain of both readings?

MLLE RUEDI: I read them as I read any dial. I am certain.

MATTHEY: Did you remain in the kitchen?

MLLE RUEDI: I stayed in the kitchen with Rosa until after the temple struck eleven.

MATTHEY: I want to ask about the repeater, the watch the master was making. Do you know how it speaks?

MLLE RUEDI: Yes. The low notes give the hours. Each quarter is a doubled note. The high notes give the minutes past the quarter. He worked on it for four years. He meant it to strike the first minute of the new century.

MATTHEY: You knew the work well.

MLLE RUEDI: I was the best hand in that atelier, Inspector, and he knew it. I do not say that with pride. It is only a fact, like a reading.

MATTHEY: Is there anything about the master that you think I should know?

MLLE RUEDI: Only that he was exact. He was exact in his work and in his habits. He was not a man who would be late for anything.

MATTHEY: Thank you, Mademoiselle. Please do not leave the house until I have spoken with the others.

MLLE RUEDI: I will not leave. I have work to finish, and it will be here.
